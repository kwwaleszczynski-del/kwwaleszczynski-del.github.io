When batteries flatten their own spreads

A 0-10 GW battery fleet dispatched against an empirical Polish supply curve from PSE data: how spreads, revenue per MW, negative-price hours and the evening peak change as storage grows.

At 2025 prices a 2-hour battery earns 308 k PLN/MW alone, 203 k at 2 GW of fleet and 72 k at 10 GW; the marginal value of the fleet turns negative near 10 GW.

Cable pooling: PV, wind and storage behind one connection

How much PV, wind and battery storage to build behind a shared grid connection in Poland: ERA5 profiles, RCE prices and an hourly LP dispatch for 856 size combinations per site, with NPV, LCOE and curtailment.

Best oversizing is 1.75-2.25x the connection without a battery and 2.75-3x with one, curtailing 9-20 % of the energy.

Battery ageing vs capacity market obligation

LFP ageing under real RCE arbitrage and capped-cycling profiles against a 17-year Polish capacity market contract: when the battery falls short, and overbuild, augmentation or module replacement compared in NPV and risk.

Built to nominal energy, a battery misses its obligation in year one; +20 % headroom lasts 7-10 years, and full arbitrage leaves 62-68 % SoH after 17 years.

PV fault detection: autoencoder vs baselines

A PyTorch autoencoder against a PR threshold, an ASTM E2848 regression and a pvlib model, scored per fault type on 20 injected faults and on real PVDAQ data.

F1 0.77 against 0.37-0.48 for the baselines; on real data it caught an inverter outage reported 10 days late by the inverter itself.

Solar PV

PV thermal check: modules, inverter, transformer

Module temperature models (Faiman, SAPM, PVsyst, Ross) fitted on PVDAQ data with cell temperature recovered from open-circuit voltage, temperature-corrected PR, sensor-fault detection, inverter and transformer thermal checks, and losses moved to Poland.

The back-of-module sensor saw only about half of the real heating: thermal losses are 10.5 % a year, not 6.7 %; temperature-corrected PR cuts the seasonal spread from 13.1 to 4.8 pp.

Capacity credit of wind and PV in Poland

Multi-state Markov models of wind and PV from ERA5 at 29 points, sequential Monte Carlo LOLE/EENS on IEEE RTS-79 and real PSE demand, and an ELCC map of Poland: the method of the author's MSc thesis rebuilt on open data.

Reproduces the IEEE RTS-79 reference LOLE exactly; wind ELCC about 26.5 % and PV about 10 %, compared with capacity market derating factors.

How good is ERA5 in Poland?

ERA5 and CERRA reanalyses checked against 19 IMGW-PIB synoptic stations and Kelmarsh hub-height wind: bias by season and hour, the error in energy, and long-term (MCP) correction.

ERA5 overstates 10 m wind by 8 % (median), about 15 % in wind energy; the matrix MCP method cuts the energy error from 41 % to 5 %.
